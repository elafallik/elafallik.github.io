This paper asks the question - How can we preform efficient inference and learning in Bayesian networks in the present of continuous latent variables, and large datasets?

# Variational Inference

## The Setup

We have a large dataset of N i.i.d samples generated by some random process involving unobserved variables: 

1.  $z\sim p(z)$  - prior
2.  $x\sim p(x\mid z)$ - likelihood

We can represent this process using a minimal Bayesian network:

![alt text](/assets/img/Auto-Encoding-Variational-Bayes/Screenshot_2024-05-16_at_15.38.41.png)

We want to infer on the posterior $p(z\mid x)$ - the probability of the hidden variables given the data - upstream inference in the network.

## The Problem

The main problem is that to calculate the posterior 

$p(z\mid x)=\frac{p(x,z)}{p(x)}$

we need to calculate the marginal likelihood 

$p(x)=\int_z p(x,z)dz$

This integration is usually challenging and computationally expensive (except in the cases of conjugate priors, where the prior and likelihood have specific parametric form that give a close form to the posterior).

## The Solution

There are many approaches to accelerate posterior calculations in Bayesian networks. One method is Variational inference, where we choose some family Q of distributions (usually a parametric family $Q=\{q_\phi (z)\}$) and look for $q\in Q$ that is the closest to the posterior $p(z\mid x)$. 

![Screenshot 2024-05-16 at 15.43.41.png](Auto-Encoding%20Variational%20Bayes%200411ccd2a02f4af09c549afac3e3257b/Screenshot_2024-05-16_at_15.43.41.png)

### What does “close” mean?

We use the KL-divergence as a measure of “distance” between distributions (although this is not symmetric and therefore is not a distance metric function: $D_{KL}[p\mid \mid q] ≠ D_{KL}[q\mid \mid p]$). Specifically, we use:

$D_{KL}[q(z)\mid \mid p(z\mid x)] = \int_z q(z)\cdot log(\frac{q(z)}{p(z\mid x)})=\mathrm{E}_{q}[log(\frac{q(z)}{p(z\mid x)})]$
